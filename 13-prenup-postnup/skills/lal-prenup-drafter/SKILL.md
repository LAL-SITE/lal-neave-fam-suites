# Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Prenup / Postnup Drafter

## Purpose
Produce a complete, enforceable Florida prenuptial or postnuptial agreement as
a Word (.docx) file. Output must be clean, final, and free of alternatives,
drafting notes, or unused provisions.

---

## STEP 0 — Route: Prenup or Postnup?

Ask immediately if not already clear:

> "Are the parties **not yet married** (prenuptial) or **already married**
> (postnuptial)?"

This controls the entire legal framework.

| Type | Governing Law | Key Difference |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Prenuptial** | §61.079 Florida Premarital Agreement Act | Enforceable without separate consideration; signing is sufficient |
| **Postnuptial** | Florida contract law + Del Vecchio / Cladis / Blum lineage | Requires independent consideration beyond the marriage itself; courts apply heightened scrutiny; alimony waivers are particularly vulnerable |

**If postnup:** Flag to Angela R. Neave/Angela R. Neave at intake — postnups require a specific
consideration strategy and attorney judgment before drafting begins.
See `references/postnup-considerations.md` for FL postnup-specific rules.

---

## STEP 1 — Hard Stops (Check Before Any Intake)

**STOP and escalate to Angela R. Neave immediately if:**

- Financial disclosure is missing or either party refuses to disclose
- Execution is planned for the day of or day before the wedding (duress risk — document timing carefully; attorney must advise)
- Either party lacks independent counsel and the disparity between parties is significant
- Client describes any pressure, ultimatum, or threat in connection with signing
- Alimony waiver is requested but the requesting party has significantly superior financial resources without any balancing protection for the other party
- This is a postnup and no independent consideration has been identified

Document the hard stop reason in the file before escalating.

## STEP 2 — Intake

Collect in batches of no more than 5 questions. Use `[BRACKETS]` for anything
not yet confirmed.

### Always Required

- Party 1: full legal name, address, counsel (yes/no, name if yes)
- Party 2: full legal name, address, counsel (yes/no, name if yes)
- Planned wedding date **OR** date of marriage (if postnup)
- Date agreement will be executed (if known)
- Florida county (for execution and governing law)

### Financial Disclosure Method

Select ONE:

- **Full Disclosure** — both parties provide complete financial schedules (attach as Exhibit A and Exhibit B); OR
- **Express Waiver** — both parties waive full disclosure in writing with acknowledgment of the risk; disclosure cannot be omitted without a signed written waiver

Document which method is being used. Schedules must be prepared and attached
before execution if Full Disclosure is selected.

### Property and Assets

For each asset category, determine the parties' agreement:

| Category | Questions to Ask |
|----------|-----------------|
| Pre-marriage assets | Does each party's pre-marriage property (and all appreciation, income, proceeds) remain separate? Any exceptions? |
| Business interests | Any businesses owned? Are goodwill, enterprise value, retained earnings, and IP included in separate property? |
| Trusts / inheritances | Any trust interests or expected inheritances? Separate or marital? |
| Commingling | Will commingling for convenience convert separate to marital, or is tracing preserved? |
| Future acquired assets | What is the default character of assets acquired during the marriage? |

### Income

- Does each party's earnings/compensation during the marriage remain separate?
- How is passive income (investment returns, rental income, distributions) characterized?
- Are there any specific income-sharing arrangements agreed to?

### Alimony / Spousal Support

**Select exactly ONE structure** — do not draft multiple alternatives:

| Option | Use When |
|--------|----------|
| **Full Waiver** | Both parties voluntarily waive all alimony; must be knowing, voluntary, and supported by adequate disclosure |
| **Limitation** | Alimony capped at specific amount or duration |
| **Conditional** | Alimony triggered only by specific conditions (e.g., marriage length, disability, child status) |
| **Reservation** | Neither party waives; court retains jurisdiction; no current agreement on terms |

**Flag for attorney:** If full waiver is selected and there is a significant wealth
disparity, include high-risk enforceability protections (Section 13 of the
master system). An alimony waiver without adequate disclosure or with coercive
circumstances is the most common ground for invalidating a prenup in Florida.

### Estate Rights

- Are elective share, intestate rights, homestead rights, exempt property rights, and family allowance being waived?
- Any exceptions or carve-outs?
- Are separate estate planning documents being prepared simultaneously? (Flag if yes — attorney should coordinate.)

### Debts

- Does each party remain responsible for their own pre-existing debts?
- Are any joint debts being incurred during the marriage? How allocated?
- Full indemnification required?

### Enforcement Framework

- Attorney's fees for enforcement: **prevailing party** (Neave Family Law Group, P.A. default) OR **own fees**?
- Is a severability clause required? (Yes — always include)
- No-reliance provision? (Yes — always include)

### High-Risk Factors Present?

Check YES/NO for each:

- [ ] Significant wealth or income disparity between parties
- [ ] One party does not have independent counsel
- [ ] Agreement is being executed close to the wedding date
- [ ] One party has limited English proficiency or other comprehension concern
- [ ] Alimony waiver with weak financial justification
- [ ] Either party has previously expressed reluctance to sign

If any YES: **High-Risk Protections (Section VII of clause library) are
MANDATORY** — include voluntariness reinforcement, fairness acknowledgment,
disparity awareness, and timing acknowledgment language.

## STEP 3 — Document Structure and Sections

Generate the agreement using the clause library embedded in this skill.
For each section: determine whether present → select one structure → insert
clause(s) → remove unused alternatives.

**See `references/clause-library.md` for all clause language.**

### Required Sections (Always Present)

1. **PARTIES AND RECITALS** — full legal names, addresses, relationship
   status, planned/actual marriage date, county, purpose of agreement
2. **DEFINITIONS** — Separate Property, Marital Property, Commingling,
   Tracing (define all terms used in the agreement)
3. **FINANCIAL DISCLOSURE** — either Full Disclosure (reference schedules)
   or Express Written Waiver
4. **PROPERTY AND ASSETS** — Section I clauses; must define Separate Property,
   tracing method, and commingling rule; include business clause if applicable;
   include trust clause if applicable
5. **INCOME AND FINANCIAL FLOW** — Section II clauses; classify earnings,
   passive income, and investment returns
6. **DEBTS AND INDEMNIFICATION** — Section V clauses; assign pre-existing
   and marital debts; full indemnification
7. **ENFORCEMENT AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK** — Section VI clauses; fee selection;
   severability; no-reliance; governing law (Florida); survivability of
   provisions despite changed circumstances
8. **ENTIRE AGREEMENT / INTEGRATION** — agreement supersedes all prior
   negotiations and understandings
9. **AMENDMENT** — amendments only by written instrument signed with same
   formalities
10. **INDEPENDENT COUNSEL** — each party acknowledges opportunity to retain
    independent counsel; acknowledges they have read and understand the agreement
11. **EXECUTION BLOCK** — both parties; two witnesses per party; notary
    acknowledgment per party (required for recording and enforcement)

### Conditional Sections

| Section | Include When |
|---------|-------------|
| **ALIMONY / SPOUSAL SUPPORT** — Section III clause | Always if alimony is addressed; omit ONLY if parties explicitly agree to say nothing about support (unusual — flag) |
| **ESTATE AND DEATH RIGHTS** — Section IV clause | When elective share, intestate rights, or homestead rights are being addressed |
| **BUSINESS INTERESTS** | When either party owns a business |
| **TRUST / INHERITANCE INTERESTS** | When either party has trust or inheritance interests |
| **HIGH-RISK ENFORCEABILITY PROTECTIONS** — Section VII clause | When any high-risk factor is present (see Step 2) |
| **CONSIDERATION CLAUSE** | Postnups ONLY — must identify specific consideration beyond the marriage; see `references/postnup-considerations.md` |
| **SCHEDULES / FINANCIAL DISCLOSURE EXHIBITS** | When Full Disclosure method is selected |

## STEP 4 — Postnup-Specific Requirements

**Read `references/postnup-considerations.md` before drafting any postnup.**

Key postnup rules:
- Florida postnups are NOT governed by §61.079 — governed by general contract law
- Courts apply **heightened scrutiny** — agreement must be fair and reasonable
  at time of execution AND at time of enforcement (*Del Vecchio v. Del Vecchio*)
- Independent consideration beyond the marriage is required — identify it explicitly
- Alimony waivers in postnups are the highest-risk provision — courts have
  struck them more readily than in prenups; attorney judgment required before including
- Both parties should have independent counsel (not just recommended — flag as
  strong risk factor if either party is unrepresented)
- Full financial disclosure is effectively mandatory — waiver is much weaker in
  postnup context
