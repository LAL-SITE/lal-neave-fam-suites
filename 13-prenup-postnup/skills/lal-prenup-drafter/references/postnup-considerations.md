# Florida Postnuptial Agreement — Legal Framework and Considerations

## Governing Law

Postnuptial agreements in Florida are NOT governed by §61.079 (the Florida
Premarital Agreement Act). They are governed by general Florida contract law,
as modified by a line of Florida case law establishing heightened scrutiny.

**Key cases (internal use — cite only if confirmed in project files):**
- *Del Vecchio v. Del Vecchio* (1962) — foundational: postnup must be fair
  and reasonable at time of execution; court may look at fairness at enforcement
- *Cladis v. Cladis* — independent consideration required beyond the marriage
- *Blum v. Blum* — reinforces consideration and disclosure requirements

---

## Key Differences from Prenups

| Issue | Prenup | Postnup |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Governing law | §61.079 | Contract law + case law |
| Consideration required | No (statute) | YES — must be identified |
| Scrutiny level | Standard enforceability | Heightened scrutiny |
| Alimony waiver risk | Moderate | HIGH — courts more readily strike |
| Disclosure standard | Required or written waiver | Effectively mandatory |
| Counsel recommendation | Strong | Near-mandatory for enforceability |
| Fairness at enforcement | Not a standalone ground | Can be examined at enforcement |

## Consideration Requirements

A postnup must have independent consideration beyond the continuation of the
marriage itself. "I won't divorce you if you sign" is NOT adequate consideration.

**Acceptable consideration examples:**
- Transfer of specific property or assets to the other party
- Payment of money or satisfaction of a debt
- One party giving up a claim or right they otherwise possess
- Specific financial benefit provided contemporaneously with signing
- A mutual exchange of legally cognizable rights

**Attorney judgment required:** Identify the specific consideration and document
it in the agreement before drafting. If no adequate consideration exists, do
not draft — escalate to Angela R. Neave.

## Alimony Waivers in Postnups

Florida courts have been especially skeptical of alimony waivers in postnups,
particularly when:
- The waiving party has significantly less financial resources
- The waiving party did not have independent counsel
- The waiver was executed under economic pressure or relationship strain
- No meaningful benefit was provided to the waiving party

**Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Default:** If a postnup includes an alimony waiver, require attorney

## Independent Counsel

Both parties should have independent counsel for postnups. If either party
is unrepresented:
- Document the opportunity to obtain counsel in the agreement itself
- Flag to the attorney for decision on whether to proceed
- Include enhanced voluntariness language

---

## Disclosure in Postnups

While §61.079 allows express waiver of disclosure in prenups, postnup case
law is less forgiving of disclosure waivers. Best practice:
- Full financial schedules attached as exhibits for postnups
- If waiver used: include detailed knowing-and-voluntary acknowledgment language
- Flag any disclosure waiver to the attorney

---

## Drafting Checklist for Postnups

Before drafting:
- [ ] Specific consideration identified and confirmed
- [ ] Attorney has approved consideration as legally adequate
- [ ] Alimony provision (if any) approved by attorney
- [ ] Both parties have or will have opportunity for independent counsel
- [ ] Full financial disclosure prepared or express waiver strategy confirmed

In the agreement:
- [ ] Consideration clause — name the specific consideration
- [ ] Full financial disclosure or enhanced waiver acknowledgment
- [ ] Enhanced voluntariness and fairness acknowledgment (Section VII always)
- [ ] Both parties' counsel identified (or opportunity to retain stated)
- [ ] Governing law: Florida
- [ ] Two witnesses and notary per party
