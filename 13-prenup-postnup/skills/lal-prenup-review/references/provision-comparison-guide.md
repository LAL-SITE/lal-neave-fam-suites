# Provision Comparison Guide: Agreement vs. Default Florida Law

Use this to assess what each provision does relative to what FL law
would provide without an agreement. For each provision the client is
evaluating, compare the agreement outcome to the default outcome.

---

## Property Division

| Scenario | Default FL Law (No Agreement) | What a Prenup Can Do |
|----------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Pre-marriage property | Non-marital; stays with owner | Confirm, expand, or modify |
| Appreciation on pre-marriage property | Passive appreciation = non-marital; active appreciation may be marital | Can make all appreciation separate or all marital |
| Income during marriage | Marital (generally) | Can make income separate for each party |
| Business goodwill | Professional goodwill may be marital | Can exclude business interests entirely |
| Inherited property | Non-marital if kept separate | Can confirm as separate or convert to marital |
| Commingled property | May become marital if can't be traced | Can define tracing rules |

**Assessment for client:** Does the provision give client more or less than default law?

---

## Alimony

| Scenario | Default FL Law (No Agreement) | What a Prenup Can Do |
|----------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Alimony eligibility | Court determines under §61.08 (2023 reform) | Can waive, limit, condition, or reserve |
| Durational alimony cap | Based on marriage length under statute | Can set own cap or eliminate |
| Bridge-the-gap | Court discretion | Can waive or limit |
| Modification | Substantial change in circumstances | Can restrict or preserve right |

**Critical question for client:** If the client is the lower-earning spouse and
the agreement waives alimony — what income/support does the client have
post-divorce? Compare against estimated alimony under §61.08 without agreement.

---

## Equitable Distribution

| Scenario | Default FL Law (No Agreement) | What a Prenup Can Do |
|----------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Marital assets | Split equitably (presumptively 50/50) | Can pre-distribute or modify |
| Marital debts | Split equitably | Can assign specifically |
| Business interests | Marital portion subject to equitable distribution | Can exclude entirely |
| Retirement accounts | Marital portion divided; may require QDRO | Can preserve as separate |

---

## Estate Rights

| Right | Default FL Law (No Agreement) | What a Prenup Can Do |
|-------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Elective share | 30% of elective estate (§732.2065) | Can waive |
| Homestead | Surviving spouse has homestead rights | Can waive (with proper execution) |
| Intestate share | Spouse receives significant share if no will | Can waive |
| Family allowance | Automatic right | Can waive |
| Exempt property | Automatic right | Can waive |

**Note for pre-execution review:** If the client is waiving estate rights,
advise whether the client has an estate plan in place that provides alternative
protection. Estate right waivers in prenups interact with wills and trusts.

---

## Key Questions for Client Assessment (Pre-Execution Tracks)

For each significant provision:
1. What does the agreement give the client vs. what would FL law give without it?
2. Is this fair given the parties' respective financial positions?
3. Does the client understand what they are giving up?
4. Is there any provision that should be added to protect the client?

For each significant provision (Divorce-Context Tracks):
1. What does this provision do in this divorce?
2. Does it benefit or burden the client?
3. Is there a viable challenge ground?
4. What is the realistic outcome if challenged vs. enforced?
