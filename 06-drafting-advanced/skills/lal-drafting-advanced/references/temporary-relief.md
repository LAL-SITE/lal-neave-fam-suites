# Temporary Relief Motion Drafting Reference

## What Temporary Relief Addresses
Temporary relief is sought within an existing or just-filed case to address urgent
issues while the case is pending final resolution. Common subjects:

| Relief Type | When Applicable |
|---|---|
| Temporary timesharing | When a parent is being denied contact with the child |
| Temporary child support | When client has majority timesharing or other qualifying scenario |
| Temporary alimony | When spouse qualifies and needs support during the case |
| Temporary attorney's fees | When there is an income disparity between parties |
| Exclusive use of marital home | In high-conflict situations — flag for attorney; not routine |
| Status quo order | When existing arrangements need to be preserved pending final hearing |

## When to Flag for Attorney Review
Staff should flag the following situations as potential temporary relief issues:
- Client is not seeing the child at all or timesharing has been denied
- Client has no income and needs support
- Client cannot afford attorney's fees
- There is a significant disparity in access to marital funds
- Safety concerns are present
- Parties or children are at immediate risk of relocation

## Motion Structure
```
MOTION FOR TEMPORARY RELIEF

I. INTRODUCTION
Brief 1-2 sentence statement of what is being requested and why urgency exists.

II. FACTUAL BACKGROUND
Numbered paragraphs. Chronological. Specific. Fact-based.
Cover:
- Date petition was filed / case status
- Current living / timesharing arrangement
- What has changed or is causing the need for temporary relief
- Relevant financial circumstances (if support or fees are at issue)
- Children's current situation (ages, school, living arrangements)

III. BASIS FOR RELIEF
Address each item of relief requested with its specific factual and legal basis.
(Do not fabricate legal citations — only include if training materials provide
reliable authority or attorney directs specific language.)

IV. RELIEF REQUESTED
Numbered list of each specific item of temporary relief sought. Be precise.
Include proposed schedule, amounts, or arrangements if available.

WHEREFORE, [Petitioner/Respondent] respectfully requests this Court enter an Order:
1. [Specific relief item 1]
2. [Specific relief item 2]
...
And for such other and further relief as this Court deems just and proper.
```

## Key Drafting Notes
- Temporary relief is a **motion** (filed within an existing case) — not a petition
- If the case has not yet been opened, the petition must be filed first
- Temporary child support should only be requested if the facts clearly support it —
  do not plead it automatically; confirm with attorney
- Alimony and attorney's fees allegations should include income disparity facts
- Financial figures must come from the file or client-provided info; use brackets
  for unconfirmed figures

## Status Quo Orders
A status quo order preserves the existing arrangement until further order of court.
Common uses:
- Preventing disposal of marital assets
- Maintaining existing timesharing while case proceeds
- Preventing relocation during the case
Flag for attorney whether to request by motion or to propose a consent order.
