# Paternity Petition Drafting Reference

## Paternity Framework (Narrower Than Divorce)
Paternity cases address:
- **P**arenting — timesharing, parental responsibility
- **C**hild support
- **E**verything else — attorney's fees, related relief

There is no equitable distribution or alimony in a paternity case.

## Required Initial Packet (New Paternity Case)
- Petition to Establish Paternity (or Petition to Establish or Disestablish Paternity if applicable)
- Civil Cover Sheet
- UCCJA / UCCJEA (children are always involved)
- Summons

## Required Allegations — Petition to Establish Paternity
1. **Jurisdiction** — Florida residence of Petitioner or that the child resides in Florida
2. **Venue** — County where one party or the child resides
3. **Child's name and DOB** — Full legal name; date of birth; state of birth
4. **Paternity allegation** — That Petitioner is the biological father (or that Respondent is) — state the basis
5. **Parenting plan / timesharing** — What the client is requesting
6. **Parental responsibility** — Sole or shared; if shared, decision-making structure
7. **Child support** — Address even if waiving; must be pled
8. **Attorney's fees** — If requesting, plead it
9. **UCCJA / UCCJEA** — Where child has lived for past 6 months

## Petition to Establish OR Disestablish Paternity
Use when there is a prior establishment of paternity being challenged, or when
genetic testing results need to be addressed. This is a distinct form — confirm
which version is appropriate before drafting.

Key inputs needed:
- Whether a prior order establishing paternity exists
- Whether genetic/DNA testing has been performed (and results if available)
- Nature of the challenge (fraud, duress, mistake, etc. — flag for attorney)

## Voluntary Acknowledgment of Paternity (VAP)
If parties signed a VAP at the hospital, note this in the petition. A VAP can
establish paternity without court involvement but may affect the type of action
needed. Flag for attorney if VAP is in the record.

## Service
- New paternity case → summons required
- Respondent has 20 days to respond after service
- If Respondent is avoiding service → flag for attorney (diligent search / publication
  procedures may apply; note potential impact of HB 775 / July 2023 law change for
  paternity by publication cases — confirm current procedure with attorney)
