# Contempt and Enforcement Drafting Reference

## Two Tracks: Civil vs. Criminal Contempt
**Civil contempt** — most common in family law. Purpose is to coerce compliance,
not punish. Available when a party has the ability to comply but has not.

**Criminal contempt** — punitive. Requires stricter procedural protections.
Most family law contempt is civil. Flag for attorney if criminal contempt is
being considered.

## Common Contempt Scenarios in Family Law
- Failure to pay child support as ordered
- Failure to pay alimony as ordered
- Violation of timesharing order (denying access, taking child without authorization)
- Failure to produce mandatory disclosure / discovery
- Failure to comply with property division order
- Failure to pay attorney's fees as ordered
- Violation of injunction or restraining order

## Required Elements — Motion for Contempt
For civil contempt, the motion must establish:
1. A **valid, existing court order** that is clear and specific
2. The **Respondent had knowledge** of the order
3. The **Respondent failed to comply** with the specific terms
4. The **Respondent had the ability to comply** (for coercive civil contempt)

## Motion for Contempt Structure
```
MOTION FOR CONTEMPT / MOTION FOR CIVIL CONTEMPT

I. INTRODUCTION
Identify the order being violated. State the date it was entered and what it requires.

II. FACTUAL BACKGROUND
Numbered paragraphs covering:
- Date and title of the controlling order
- Specific obligations imposed by the order
- Date(s) noncompliance began
- Specific acts or omissions constituting noncompliance
- Any prior attempts to resolve informally (if applicable)
- Amount of arrears / extent of noncompliance (use specific figures from the record)

III. RELIEF REQUESTED
WHEREFORE, [party] respectfully requests this Court:
1. Set this matter for an evidentiary hearing
2. Find [Respondent] in contempt of court
3. [Enter a purge amount / purge conditions] [for support arrears]
4. Award attorney's fees and costs incurred in bringing this motion
5. [Any other specific relief — incarceration language goes here ONLY if
   attorney has authorized it and ability to pay is established in the record]
```

## Support Arrears — Specificity Required
For support contempt motions, must include:
- The amount ordered per month/week (and date order was entered)
- Payments made (if any) with dates and amounts
- Total arrearage as of a specific date
- Source for these figures (order, payment records, accounting — cite what's in the file)
Do not estimate. Use only figures supported by the record. Use brackets for any
figures that must be confirmed.

## Purge Amounts
A purge amount is what the contemnor must pay to avoid or be released from
incarceration. Must be based on the contemnor's **ability to pay** at the time of
the hearing — not the total arrearage. Flag for attorney to set the purge amount
at the hearing or in the motion if directed.

## Motion to Enforce vs. Motion for Contempt
- **Motion to Enforce** — used when compliance is sought without contempt finding;
  softer approach; does not require showing willful noncompliance
- **Motion for Contempt** — requires showing of willful noncompliance; stronger
  remedy; can result in incarceration
Attorney should confirm which vehicle to use based on facts and strategy.

## Attorney's Fees on Contempt Motions
Include a request for attorney's fees in every contempt motion. The prevailing
party on a contempt motion may be entitled to fees. Plead it even if uncertain
— you can always withdraw it.
