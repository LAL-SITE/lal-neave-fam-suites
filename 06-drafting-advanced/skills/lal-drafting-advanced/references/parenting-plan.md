# Parenting Plan Drafting Reference

## What a Parenting Plan Must Cover
Every parenting plan in Florida must address, at minimum:
1. **Parental responsibility** — Shared (joint) or sole; if shared, how decisions are made
2. **Timesharing schedule** — Regular weekly/biweekly schedule; specify days and times
3. **Holiday / vacation schedule** — Every major holiday; summer; school breaks; birthdays
   (if parties agree)
4. **Decision-making** — Education, healthcare, extracurricular activities, religious upbringing
5. **Communication** — How parties communicate about the child; how child communicates
   with the non-primary parent
6. **Transportation** — Who provides pickup/dropoff; where exchanges occur
7. **Travel** — Domestic travel notice requirements; international travel consent

## Parental Responsibility Options
| Option | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Shared parental responsibility | Both parents make major decisions jointly; default in Florida |
| Shared PR with ultimate authority | Both consult, but one parent has final say in a specific domain |
| Sole parental responsibility | One parent makes all decisions; requires specific findings; flag for attorney |

## Timesharing Schedule Options
- 50/50 equal timesharing (week on / week off; 2-2-3; other rotations)
- Primary residence with one parent + specified visitation for the other
- Supervised visitation (requires specific factual support; flag for attorney)
- Virtual / long-distance timesharing (if parents are in different cities)

Specify: exact days, pickup/dropoff times, exchange location.

## Holiday Schedule Framework
At minimum address:
- New Year's Eve / New Year's Day
- Martin Luther King Jr. Day
- Presidents' Day / Memorial Day / Labor Day
- Spring Break (school calendar)
- Mother's Day / Father's Day (typically to the respective parent)
- Independence Day
- Thanksgiving (typically alternating)
- Winter / Christmas Break (typically split or alternating)
- Child's Birthday (many plans split or alternate)
- Parents' Birthdays (optional)
- Each parent's extended family holidays (optional)

State whether holidays **override** the regular schedule or are in addition to it.
State the default if a holiday is not listed.

## Summer Vacation
Address:
- How summer is divided between parents
- Extended vacation blocks (how much notice is required to designate)
- Deadline to designate summer schedule (e.g., by April 1 of each year)

## Communication Between Parents
- Preferred method (app such as TalkingParents, OurFamilyWizard, text, email)
- Response time expectation
- Notification requirements for emergencies
- Right of first refusal (if applicable — address separately; see below)

## Right of First Refusal (ROFR)
If included: specify the threshold (hours of absence), method of notice, and
response window. ROFR is not automatic — it must be specifically negotiated and
pled. Flag for attorney whether to include.

## School and Educational Information
- Which school the child attends
- How school-related decisions are made
- Both parents receive school records, report cards, school communications
- Both parents may attend school events independently

## Medical / Healthcare
- Who carries health insurance
- How uninsured expenses are divided (typically 50/50 in shared responsibility cases)
- Emergency decision-making
- Both parents can access medical records and speak with providers

## Relocation Language
Include standard relocation language: neither parent may relocate the child's
primary residence more than 50 miles from the current address without written
agreement of the other parent or court order.

## Attorney Review Notes for Parenting Plans
- Right of first refusal threshold requires attorney input
- Supervised visitation language requires attorney review before including
- Out-of-state parent schedules should be reviewed by attorney for feasibility
- Any deviation from 50/50 timesharing should be flagged with attorney for
  best interests analysis support
