# Injunctions / Domestic Violence / Protective Orders Drafting Reference

## Types of Injunctions in Florida Family Law
| Type | Statute | Relationship Required |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic Violence | § 741.30 | Family or household member, spouse, former spouse, parent of common child |
| Repeat Violence | § 784.046 | Any person (two incidents of violence or stalking) |
| Dating Violence | § 784.046 | Person in current or recent dating relationship |
| Sexual Violence | § 784.046 | Any victim of sexual violence |
| Stalking | § 784.0485 | Any person subject to stalking or cyberstalking |

## Service Rule for Injunctions
**Injunctions must be served by the SHERIFF — not by a private process server.**
This is a hard rule. Note it in every injunction-related draft.

## Domestic Violence Injunction — Typical Forms Packet
Florida Supreme Court-approved forms are used for injunctions. Do not draft
from scratch — use the approved forms. Identify the correct forms package and
flag for the paralegal handling the filing.

Standard DV Packet includes:
- Petition for Injunction for Protection Against Domestic Violence (Form 12.980(b)(1))
- Affidavit in Support
- Temporary Injunction Order (completed by judge, not staff)
- Final Judgment of Injunction (for hearing use)

## Petition Allegations for Domestic Violence Injunction
The petition must allege:
1. The relationship between the parties (as defined by the statute)
2. The specific act(s) of domestic violence — describe with dates, locations, and
   what occurred; be specific and factual
3. Imminent danger — the petitioner is in fear of becoming a victim of domestic violence
   or has been a victim
4. Prior history of violence (if applicable) — prior incidents strengthen the petition
5. Presence of minor children and whether they witnessed or were present during violence
6. Whether firearms are present in the home

## What the Petition Should NOT Do
- Do not exaggerate or embellish facts
- Do not include legal conclusions without factual support
- Do not omit incidents — include all relevant incidents even if they seem minor
- Do not describe what the petitioner "feels" without supporting facts

## Temporary Injunction (Ex Parte)
After the petition is filed, the judge may enter a temporary injunction without notice
to the Respondent if the petition is sufficient on its face. A final hearing will be
set, typically within 15 days. This is done by the judge — staff does not draft the
temporary order.

## Final Hearing Preparation
At the final hearing the judge will determine whether to enter a permanent injunction.
Flag for attorney:
- Whether the petitioner has witnesses
- Whether there is documented evidence (photos, messages, medical records, police reports)
- Whether the Respondent is expected to appear and contest

## Other Injunction Notes
- **Repeat Violence** requires TWO incidents — one is not enough unless it constitutes sexual violence
- **Stalking injunctions** (§ 784.0485) address cyberstalking as well
- **Dating violence** requires that the parties be or were recently in a romantic relationship
  (not just acquaintances or coworkers)
- All injunction matters should be reviewed by an attorney before filing
