GAL Source Interview Processor for Florida Guardian ad Litem matters. Always run this skill to document each interview before moving to the next activity.

# GAL Source Interview Processor

## Purpose

This skill takes raw notes, a verbal debrief, or a transcript from any source interview
and produces a **structured Source Summary** — properly attributed, categorized, and
analysis-ready — that can be quoted directly into any GAL report.

Every source type — parent, child, collateral, or professional — processes through
the same framework with source-specific analytical layers applied on top.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Source Type

| Source Type | Who It Covers |
|-------------|--------------|
| **PARENT** | Mother, Father, Step-parent with significant parenting role |
| **CHILD** | Any minor child subject to or involved in the case |
| **COLLATERAL — LAY** | Grandparents, relatives, neighbors, prior partners, family friends |
| **COLLATERAL — PROFESSIONAL** | Teachers, school counselors, coaches, pediatricians |
| **COLLATERAL — CLINICAL** | Therapists, psychiatrists, psychologists, treatment providers |
| **COLLATERAL — LEGAL/SYSTEM** | Prior parenting coordinators, DCF workers, law enforcement |

Default to the most specialized category when a source could fit multiple types.
A therapist who is also a family member → COLLATERAL — CLINICAL.

---

## Step 2 — Capture Core Interview Data

For every source type, record:

- **Source Name / Role:** (name and relationship to the case)
- **Date(s) of Contact:**
- **Method:** In person / Phone / Video / Email / Combination
- **Location:** (if in person — office, home visit, school, etc.)
- **Duration:** (approximate)
- **Others Present:** (if any — note who and why)
- **GAL's Initial Credibility Impression:** HIGH / MODERATE / GUARDED / LOW
  *(internal working note — not for the report)*

---

## Step 3 — Apply Source-Specific Processing Framework

---

### FRAMEWORK A: PARENT INTERVIEW

Process through all four lenses. Each produces a separate output block.

#### A1 — Factual Account
Summarize what the parent reported in neutral, source-attributed language.
- Use: "Mother reported…" / "Father stated…" / "Father denied…"
- Categorize each item: ALLEGATION / REPORTED STATEMENT / DOCUMENTED (if verifiable)
- Do not adopt the parent's framing. Restate in neutral terms.
- Flag any factual claim verifiable against existing records.

#### A2 — Self-Awareness and Accountability Assessment
*(Internal analytical note — informs report language but is not quoted verbatim)*

Assess where this parent falls on the accountability spectrum per the Advanced GAL
Training materials: at one end, the parent who identifies everything as the other's fault
and contributes nothing to the problem; at the other, the parent who names specific
things they have done, acknowledges them, and describes what they are doing about it.

- Did the parent name any specific way their own conduct contributed to the conflict?
- Were concessions genuine and specific, or general and immediately pivoted away from?
- Did the parent accurately represent facts unflattering to themselves?
- Was accountability performed (rehearsed, general, pivot to other parent) or genuine
  (specific, unprompted, uncomfortable)?

⚠️ Performed accountability sounds different from genuine accountability. Genuine is
specific, unprompted, and uncomfortable. Performed is general, rehearsed, and pivots
immediately back to what the other parent did.

Document specific behavioral examples. Do not label the parent. Describe the behavior.

> Example: "When asked directly whether there was anything she would do differently,
> Mother identified [specific example] and acknowledged that [specific conduct] was not
> appropriate. She did not redirect to Father's conduct when doing so."

#### A3 — Child-Centered vs. Parent-Centered Framing Assessment
*(Internal analytical note)*

- When discussing outcomes, whose experience did this parent center — the child's or their own?
- Did this parent acknowledge the child's need for a relationship with the other parent?
- Could this parent hold both truths: concern about the other parent AND acknowledgment
  that the child loves that parent?
- What was the parent willing to sacrifice for the child's benefit?

Document specific statements from the interview illustrating the framing pattern.

#### A4 — Credibility as a Historian
*(Internal analytical note)*

- Does the account contain verifiable specifics, or is it uniformly self-favorable?
- Did the parent accurately represent documented events, even unflattering ones?
  (A parent who accurately represents unflattering facts earns credibility.)
- Does the account track with collateral sources who had no opportunity to coordinate?
- Identify any specific inconsistency between this account and: (a) documents reviewed,
  (b) the other parent's account, (c) collateral accounts already obtained.

---

### FRAMEWORK B: CHILD INTERVIEW

Child interviews require heightened care. Apply these rules strictly.

#### B1 — Pre-Interview Setup (Document)
- Child's age and developmental stage
- Where interview took place; who was present (neither parent should be present or audible)
- How the GAL introduced themselves and the purpose of the interview
- Whether the child was told it is okay to say "I don't know," "I don't remember," or "I don't understand"
- This permission reduces social pressure that produces fabricated or embellished responses

#### B2 — Interview Arc and Method
- How did the GAL move from rapport-building to substantive areas?
- Were questions open-ended or leading? Note any leading questions used and flag them.
- What did the child volunteer vs. respond to direct questions?
  **Volunteered disclosures carry more independent weight — flag them explicitly.**

#### B3 — Factual Account
Use: "The child stated…" / "The child disclosed…"
- Apply strict source category labels
- Separate verbal statements from behavioral observations during the interview
- Note affect: did the child's emotional tone match the content?
  Disconnects between content and affect are clinically significant.

#### B4 — Stated Preference (if expressed)
Document any stated preference regarding timesharing, living arrangements, or contact.

Assess preference quality across four dimensions:
1. **Authenticity** — Does this feel like the child's own preference, or does it track
   a parent's litigation narrative too closely?
2. **Consistency** — Expressed consistently across multiple adults and settings, or new
   and context-specific?
3. **Origin analysis** — Evidence of coaching? Does the child's language sound like a child
   or like an adult? Does the "why" track the other parent's talking points exactly?
4. **Alienated vs. frightened** — A child who is frightened of a parent needs safety
   interventions. A child who is alienated from a parent needs different interventions.
   These are not the same. Do not conflate them.

#### B5 — Behavioral Observations
Document the child's demeanor, affect, and behavior during the interview. Note any
before/after behavioral patterns reported by collaterals or records that corroborate
or contradict the child's verbal account.

#### B6 — People-Pleaser Flag
Did this child appear to be managing their responses for approval? Signs include:
- Uniformly positive accounts of both parents despite documented conflict
- Rapid agreement with any reframing the GAL offered
- Physical signs of distress despite verbal claims of "everything is fine"
- Account that shifted when the GAL expressed mild skepticism

If flagged: note that the verbal account may underrepresent the child's actual experience
and that behavioral data should be weighted more heavily than verbal account.

---

### FRAMEWORK C: COLLATERAL — LAY WITNESS

#### C1 — Relationship and Bias Assessment
- What is this witness's relationship to each party?
- Who referred this witness? (Parent referral → potential bias)
- What is their stake in the outcome?
- Bias does not disqualify a witness — it is a filter. What is useful despite the bias?

#### C2 — Firsthand vs. Secondhand Separation
**This is the most critical step for collateral witnesses.**

For everything the witness reported, classify:
- **FIRSTHAND** — Personally saw, heard, or experienced
- **SECONDHAND** — Told by someone else; not directly witnessed

Only firsthand accounts go into the report as attributed source material.
Secondhand accounts may inform analysis but must be labeled as such and carry no
independent evidentiary weight.

#### C3 — Factual Account (Firsthand Only)
Summarize in source-attributed neutral language.
Use: "According to [Name/Role], who was present at the time…"
Anchor each account to a specific time period where possible.

#### C4 — Independent Convergence Check
Does this witness's firsthand account corroborate or contradict:
- Either parent's account?
- The child's account?
- Other collateral accounts?
- Documentary evidence?

**Independent convergence — same account from sources with no opportunity to coordinate —
is the strongest credibility signal in the investigation. Flag it explicitly when present.**

#### C5 — Reluctance or Fear
Note if this witness expressed reluctance or fear of a party. Document whether the GAL
explained the hearsay waiver and the GAL's testimony process.

---

### FRAMEWORK D: COLLATERAL — PROFESSIONAL OR CLINICAL

All Framework C steps apply. Additionally:

#### D1 — Role Classification
Is this provider:
- **Treating provider** — Clinical relationship; obligation runs to the patient; findings
  subject to privilege; cannot be introduced without consent
- **Forensic evaluator** — Obligation runs to the court; findings are evidentiary;
  collateral sources reviewed; admissible

⚠️ Do not treat a treating provider's clinical impressions as equivalent to a forensic
evaluation. Note which type this source is — it matters for how findings are used.

#### D2 — Clinical Observations (Document Carefully)
Distinguish:
- Formal clinical findings / diagnoses (from records or stated directly)
- Clinical impressions from treatment (therapeutic relationship, not forensic — weight accordingly)
- Behavioral observations that corroborate or contradict other sources

#### D3 — Scope of Knowledge
- What period does this provider's knowledge cover?
- How frequently have they seen the child/party?
- What have they NOT observed that the GAL still needs to fill from other sources?

#### D4 — Evaluation Flags
If an evaluation is warranted based on this provider's information, flag it:
- Type of evaluation needed (forensic psychological / substance abuse / psycho-educational)
- What the evaluation should address
- What collateral sources the evaluator should review
- Note: evaluation orders must specify "forensic psychological evaluation" — not just "psychological evaluation"

---

## Output Standards

- Every factual claim attributed to this source specifically
- Allegations remain allegations — never converted to facts
- Firsthand and secondhand accounts separated for all collateral sources
- Analytical notes clearly flagged as internal — not for direct report quotation
- Do not adopt any source's narrative framing
- Behavioral observations and volunteered disclosures are more significant than
  prompted verbal responses — always note which is which
- If this interview opens a new gap, name it and add it to the tracker
