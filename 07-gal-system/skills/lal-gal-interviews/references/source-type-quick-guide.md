## Source Type Decision Tree

When you receive information, ask: **Who or what is the source?**

---

### Type 1 — Party Statement (Mother or Father)

**What it is:** Information provided directly by one of the parties to the litigation.

**How to attribute:** "Mother reported…" / "Father stated…" / "Mother alleged…"

**Weight and handling:**
- Treat as **REPORTED STATEMENT** until corroborated
- Never elevate to fact based on one party's account alone
- Note any internal inconsistency within the party's own account
- Note any inconsistency with records or other sources
- Include contrary accounts from the other party where they exist
- Apply behavioral specificity — what exactly did the party say or do?

**Credibility signals to track:**
- [ ] Consistent across interviews?
- [ ] Consistent with documents?
- [ ] Independently corroborated?
- [ ] Internal contradictions present?

---

### Type 2 — Child Statement

**What it is:** Something the child communicated to the GAL directly.

**How to attribute:** "The child disclosed…" / "The child stated…" / "During the interview, the child expressed…"

**Weight and handling:**
- Age and developmental stage affect interpretation — always note
- Never treat as proof of what the child alleges without corroboration
- Note spontaneity (unprompted) vs. responsiveness (in answer to question)
- Note the setting and who was present
- Note any signs of coaching, rehearsal, or adult language in the child's account
- Children may genuinely believe things that are not factually accurate
- Contradictions between child's statements and records or collaterals must be noted

---

### Type 3 — Collateral Professional

**What it is:** A licensed professional with direct knowledge of the child or family.
Examples: therapist, pediatrician, school counselor, psychologist, teacher.

**How to attribute:** "According to [Name], [credential/role]…" / "[Name] reported…"

**Weight and handling:**
- Generally higher credibility than lay collaterals due to professional training
- Note the professional's direct vs. indirect knowledge (observed vs. reported to them)
- Note any dual relationship (treating therapist vs. evaluator)
- Distinguish between clinical observations and a party's self-report to the professional
- Records are generally more reliable than recalled verbal statements

---

### Type 4 — Lay Collateral

**What it is:** A non-professional with personal knowledge of the child or family.
Examples: grandparent, neighbor, family friend, coach, religious leader.

**How to attribute:** "[Name], [relationship to child/family], stated…"

**Weight and handling:**
- Note the relationship — family collaterals often align with the party who provided them
- Note the basis of knowledge — how often do they see the child? In what context?
- Note any apparent bias or self-interest
- Corroboration by independent lay collaterals increases reliability
- Treat as **REPORTED STATEMENT** unless corroborated by records or independent sources

---

### Type 5 — Document / Record

**What it is:** A written record created by a third party or institution.
Examples: school records, medical records, court filings, police reports, text messages.

**How to attribute:** "School records dated [date] reflect…" / "The pediatric records from [provider] document…"

**Weight and handling:**
- Generally more reliable than recalled verbal statements
- Note the record's date relative to the events at issue
- Note whether the record reflects the provider's own observations or a party's self-report
- Text messages and emails are attributable to the sender — note context and tone neutrally
- Court filings are documents — note what is alleged, not established

---

### Type 6 — GAL Direct Observation

**What it is:** Something the GAL personally observed during a home visit, child interview, or other direct contact.

**How to attribute:** "The GAL observed…" / "During the home visit on [date], the GAL noted…"

**Weight and handling:**
- This is the GAL's own finding — not attributed to anyone else
- Describe specifically what was observed — not what it means
- Apply behavioral specificity — describe conduct, environment, and interaction
- Note what was and was not observed (absence of expected items is relevant)

## Quick Reference — Attribution Language

| Source | Template |
|--------|---------|
| Mother | "Mother reported…" / "Mother alleged…" / "Mother stated…" |
| Father | "Father reported…" / "Father alleged…" / "Father stated…" |
| Child | "The child disclosed…" / "The child stated…" |
| Professional collateral | "According to [Name], [credential]…" |
| Lay collateral | "[Name], [relationship], stated…" |
| Record | "[Record type] dated [date] reflects…" |
| GAL observation | "The GAL observed…" / "The GAL noted…" |
| Allegation only | "[Party] alleged…" — label: ALLEGATION |
| Disputed | "The parties' accounts conflict on this point…" |
