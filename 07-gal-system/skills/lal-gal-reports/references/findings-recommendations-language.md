## The Core Distinction

| FINDINGS | RECOMMENDATIONS |
|----------|----------------|
| What the evidence shows | What should happen |
| Past and present tense | Future-oriented |
| Attributed to sources | Tied to specific findings |
| Neutral, factual language | Specific and actionable |
| No advocacy | No unsupported conclusions |

**Hard rule:** A recommendation must never appear in the Findings section.
**Hard rule:** A factual assertion must never appear only in the Recommendations section.

---

## Factor-by-Factor Framework

For each applicable §61.13(3) factor, use this structure:

```
[Factor number and statutory text]

Relevant Findings:
[What the evidence shows — drawn from investigation — attributed to sources —
factual and neutral — no advocacy language]

Recommendation:
[Specific, actionable, tied to the finding above — explains why it serves
the child's best interest]
```

---

## Recommendation Writing Standard

Each recommendation MUST be:

**Specific** — names the person, action, setting, and timeline
**Traceable** — links back to a named finding in the report
**Enforceable** — written as something a court order could implement
**Explained** — states why this recommendation serves the child's best interest

### ❌ Weak Recommendation Examples

> "The parties should communicate better."
> "Father should be more involved."
> "The child needs therapy."
> "The parties should engage in co-parenting therapy."

### ✅ Strong Recommendation Examples

> "[Parent] should participate in [type of therapy] with a licensed [credential]
> specializing in [area], meeting no less than [frequency], until such time as
> [condition]. This recommendation is based on the GAL's finding that [specific
> conduct documented in the investigation]."

> "The parties should exchange the minor child at [neutral location] rather than
> at either parent's home. This recommendation is based on the GAL's observation
> that [specific documented conduct]."

## Attribution Language — Quick Reference

| Situation | Language to Use |
|-----------|----------------|
| GAL's own observation | "The GAL observed…" / "During the home visit, the GAL noted…" |
| What a parent said | "Mother reported…" / "Father stated…" / "Father denied…" |
| What a collateral said | "According to [Name/Role]…" / "[Name] reported…" |
| What a record shows | "School records reflect…" / "The therapist's notes document…" |
| An allegation | "Mother alleged…" / "Father contends…" |
| A disputed fact | "The parties' accounts conflict on this point…" |
| A corroborated fact | "Both [Source A] and [Source B] independently confirmed…" |

## Behavioral Specificity Standard

Never use labels. Describe conduct.

| ❌ Label | ✅ Behavioral Description |
|---------|--------------------------|
| "Lacks insight" | "When asked what he believed had contributed to the current conflict, [Party] consistently identified only [Other Party]'s actions and did not identify any specific way in which his own conduct had affected the dynamic." |
| "Is manipulative" | "[Party] instructed the child [specific instruction], as reported by [source]." |
| "Is unstable" | "During the home visit, the GAL observed [specific observation]. [Party] reported [specific statement]." |
| "Is a good parent" | "The home was clean and organized. The child's school materials were visible and organized. The child interacted comfortably with [Party] throughout the visit." |

## Contra-Positive Accounts — Required Protocol

Every significant contrary account must be addressed. If a source's account undermines
a finding:

**Option A:** Explain why the GAL did not find it credible:
> "While [Source] reported [contrary account], the GAL does not find this account
> credible because [specific reason — inconsistency with records, inconsistency with
> other accounts, implausibility, etc.]."

**Option B:** Acknowledge it as a genuine factual dispute for the court:
> "The parties' accounts conflict on this point. Mother stated [X]. Father stated [Y].
> The GAL was unable to independently verify which account is accurate. This is a
> factual dispute for the court to resolve."

**You may not omit a contrary account. That is not an option.**

---

## Common Drafting Failures — Checklist

Before finalizing Section IV, check for each of the following:

- [ ] No recommendation lacks a finding
- [ ] No finding lacks a source
- [ ] No allegation has been converted to a fact
- [ ] No label used in place of behavioral description
- [ ] No contrary account omitted
- [ ] No advocacy language present
- [ ] Every recommendation is specific enough to be ordered by a court
- [ ] Every recommendation explains why it serves the child's best interest
