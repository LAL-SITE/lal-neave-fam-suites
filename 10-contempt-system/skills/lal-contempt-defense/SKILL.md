# Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Contempt & Enforcement — Defense Skill

## Purpose

This skill governs how to assist Neave Family Law Group, P.A. paralegals and staff in building, drafting, and
organizing Florida family law contempt and enforcement **defenses** on behalf of a
client accused of violating a court order. The client is the respondent. The goal is
to identify every viable defense, expose every weakness in the movant's case, draft a
clean and strategic response, and prepare the client for the hearing.

**Defense posture first.** Before drafting anything, analyze the movant's case for
weaknesses. A contempt motion that fails on one element fails entirely.

---

## Step 1: Analyze the Motion Filed Against the Client

**Get and read the contempt/enforcement motion first.** You cannot build a defense
without knowing exactly what is alleged.

Request from the user:
- [ ] The motion for contempt or enforcement that was filed
- [ ] The order or parenting plan at issue
- [ ] Any hearing notice or order to show cause
- [ ] The client's version of events
- [ ] Any documents showing what the client did or did not do

Then analyze the motion against the four contempt elements:

| Element Movant Must Prove | How Strong Is Their Case? | Defense Opportunity? |
|---|---|---|
| Valid, specific court order exists | | |
| Respondent had knowledge of the order | | |
| Respondent had present ability to comply | | |
| Respondent willfully failed to comply | | |

If any element is weak, that is a defense. Identify it specifically and flag it for
the attorney.

---

## Step 2: Classify the Defense Theory

Before drafting a response, determine the primary defense posture:

| Defense Category | When It Applies |
|---|---|
| **Full compliance** | Client did comply — documentary proof exists |
| **Substantial compliance** | Client complied in material part — minor deviation excused |
| **Inability to comply** | Client lacked financial or physical ability — not willful |
| **Order is vague / ambiguous** | The order doesn't clearly require what movant claims |
| **Order has never been served** | Client cannot be in contempt of order they weren't served with |
| **Wrong vehicle** | Movant is seeking modification dressed up as enforcement |
| **Movant's own conduct** | Movant's interference or breach affects ability to comply |
| **Changed circumstances** | Compliance became impossible due to circumstances |
| **Good-faith effort** | Client attempted compliance, fell short without willfulness |
| **No harm / no damages** | Technical noncompliance but no actual harm to movant |

A defense response may combine multiple theories. But be strategic — leading with
the strongest argument protects the client's credibility.

---

## Step 3: Deep-Dive on the Most Important Defenses

### A. Inability to Comply

This is the most commonly raised and most heavily scrutinized defense for financial
contempt. The respondent bears the burden of proving inability after the movant
establishes a prima facie case.

To establish inability:
- [ ] Current income documentation (paystubs, tax returns, bank statements)
- [ ] Evidence of job loss, reduced income, or financial hardship
- [ ] Monthly expense documentation showing inability to pay support and maintain life
- [ ] Medical records if health-related inability
- [ ] Evidence of good-faith efforts to comply (partial payments, communication)

**Critical:** The court looks at ability at the time of the hearing, not just at the
time of the missed obligation. If ability has been restored, inability defense weakens.

Do not raise inability if the client has money for discretionary expenses but claims
inability to pay support or other financial obligations. That will hurt credibility.

### B. Order Vagueness / Ambiguity

If the operative language of the order does not clearly require the specific act the
movant claims was required:
- Quote the exact order language
- Identify why it is ambiguous or susceptible to more than one interpretation
- Show that the client's interpretation was reasonable and in good faith
- Note that vague orders should be clarified, not punished through contempt

**This is a structural argument** — it challenges the sufficiency of the movant's
case before reaching the client's conduct.

### C. Full or Substantial Compliance

If the client complied:
- Document every payment, exchange, or performance with dates and amounts
- Identify any discrepancy between the client's record and the movant's claims
- Show communication demonstrating efforts to comply
- If partial: explain why partial compliance occurred and what prevented full compliance

### D. Movant's Own Conduct (Unclean Hands / Interference)

If the movant interfered with the client's ability to comply:
- For parenting time: if the client tried to exercise timesharing and was denied, that
  is both a defense and a potential counterclaim
- For financial obligations: if the movant's conduct (failure to provide required
  documents, failure to cooperate in refinancing, etc.) caused the client's inability
- Document this carefully — it may flip the matter entirely

### E. Wrong Vehicle / Modification in Disguise

If what the movant is really asking for is a different arrangement than the order
requires, that is a modification issue. Contempt cannot be used to change an order.
Flag this as a legal issue requiring attorney analysis.

---

## Step 4: Counterclaim Analysis

In every contempt defense matter, analyze whether the client has cross-claims:

- Has the movant violated the same order or another order?
- Is the movant interfering with timesharing while also complaining about it?
- Are there arrears owed to the client?
- Has the movant failed to comply with financial obligations?
- Is the motion being used as leverage in a modification dispute?

If counterclaims exist, the response motion may be paired with or followed by the
client's own enforcement motion (use the movant skill for that component).

---

## Step 5: Build the Defense Chronology

The defense needs its own fact record. Do not simply react to the movant's timeline.

Build a chronology from the client's perspective that shows:
- What the order required
- What the client did and when (with documentation)
- Any communications showing good faith
- Any circumstances beyond the client's control
- Any communications the client sent that the movant ignored
- Any prior history of compliance
- Any prior instances where the movant raised similar claims without merit

Neutral, dated, documented — same standard as the movant's chronology.

---

## Step 6: Evidence Checklist (Defense)

**For financial contempt defense:**
- [ ] Proof of any payments made (bank records, wire confirmations, cancelled checks)
- [ ] Income records showing reduced or absent income (if inability defense)
- [ ] Bank statements showing financial status during period of alleged noncompliance
- [ ] Any communications acknowledging difficulty and offering to cure
- [ ] Medical or employment records (if inability is health or job-related)
- [ ] Evidence that movant refused or obstructed payment

**For parenting plan/timesharing contempt defense:**
- [ ] Exchange log or calendar showing client's timesharing attempts
- [ ] Text messages or emails showing compliance efforts or movant's interference
- [ ] Communications showing good faith scheduling
- [ ] Any documentation that the child refused or circumstances prevented exchange
- [ ] School or medical records (if scheduling conflicts are claimed)

**For financial obligation contempt defense (deed, refinancing, etc.):**
- [ ] Communications showing efforts to comply
- [ ] Documentation of third-party delays (lender, title company, court approval)
- [ ] Evidence that movant failed to cooperate with required steps
- [ ] Timeline showing when compliance was achievable and what delayed it

**For discovery contempt defense:**
- [ ] Documents actually produced (with date produced)
- [ ] Communications showing good-faith compliance efforts
- [ ] Objections filed to discovery requests
- [ ] Evidence of disputes about scope or privilege

## Step 7: Draft the Response

### Response Structure (Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Style)

```
IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF THE [COUNTY] JUDICIAL CIRCUIT
IN AND FOR [COUNTY] COUNTY, FLORIDA

IN RE: THE MATTER OF:

[PETITIONER FULL NAME],
    Petitioner,
v.
[RESPONDENT FULL NAME],
    Respondent.

Case No.: [CASE NUMBER]
Division: [DIVISION]

RESPONDENT'S RESPONSE TO MOTION FOR CONTEMPT AND ENFORCEMENT
(AND MOTION FOR AFFIRMATIVE RELIEF, IF APPLICABLE)

COMES NOW Respondent, [CLIENT NAME], by and through the undersigned [attorney /
pro se], and files this Response to [OTHER PARTY]'s Motion for Contempt and
Enforcement, and in support states:

GENERAL DENIAL / OVERVIEW PARAGRAPH
[Concise statement of the defense position — what the motion gets wrong, what the
client actually did, and what the correct outcome should be]

RESPONSE TO ALLEGATIONS
[Numbered paragraphs responding to each numbered allegation in the motion —
admit, deny, or explain with specificity]

AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES
[If applicable: inability to comply, order vagueness, substantial compliance, etc.]

COUNTERCLAIMS / AFFIRMATIVE RELIEF REQUESTED
[If applicable]

WHEREFORE, [CLIENT NAME] respectfully requests this Court:
[Specific relief: deny the motion, dismiss, award fees for frivolous filing, etc.]

CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
```

### Tone and Drafting Rules
- Measured, factual, not defensive in tone
- Do not match the other party's emotional register
- Each response paragraph keyed to the allegation being addressed
- Affirmative defenses stated clearly and factually
- Every defense tied to specific evidence that exists or can be obtained

---

## Step 8: Proposed Order (Defense Version)

If the client has a strong defense or counterclaims, draft a proposed order that:
- Denies the motion for contempt
- Makes findings on the specific defense (inability, compliance, etc.)
- Grants any counterclaims or fee awards if supported
- Sets compliance terms going forward (to avoid a repeat)

---

## Step 9: Hearing Preparation Checklist (Defense)

- [ ] Copy of the motion filed against the client
- [ ] Copy of the order at issue
- [ ] All payment records, receipts, or confirmation of compliance
- [ ] Exchange logs, calendar, or text record (if parenting time)
- [ ] Income/expense records (if inability defense)
- [ ] Key communications in chronological order — printed
- [ ] Proposed order defending and requesting any affirmative relief
- [ ] Client prepped: how to answer questions about the order, what they did, why
- [ ] Client advised: incarceration is on the table if ability exists and court is
  convinced of willfulness — stress the importance of demonstrating good faith
- [ ] Fee award analysis prepared (was the motion frivolous or brought in bad faith?)

## Red Flags — Stop and Flag These

- **Client has no documentation of compliance.** A bare denial without evidence will
  not survive. Identify what records exist and what is missing before drafting.

- **Client has money but claims inability.** This will destroy credibility and likely
  fail. Flag for attorney — a different defense or negotiated resolution may be better.

- **Client's story contradicts the order.** If the client says the order means
  something different than the plain language suggests, flag for attorney review.
  Contempt findings are serious — do not oversell a weak defense.

- **Multiple prior contempt findings.** A pattern of contempt findings changes the
  hearing posture significantly. Advise the attorney before drafting.

- **Incarceration is actually possible.** If the motion includes a request for
  incarceration and the client has present ability to comply, flag this prominently.
  The client needs to understand the stakes and the hearing must be treated as critical.

- **Client violated the order and knows it.** Advise attorney. The question becomes
  negotiated resolution or cure, not defense strategy.

- **Motion is clearly a tactical filing.** Flag for attorney — motions brought in bad
  faith may support a fee award against the movant.
