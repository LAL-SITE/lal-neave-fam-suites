# Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Contempt & Enforcement — Movant Skill

## Purpose

This skill governs how to assist Neave Family Law Group, P.A. paralegals and staff in building, drafting, and
organizing Florida family law contempt and enforcement matters **on behalf of the
client seeking to enforce a prior court order**. The client is the movant. The goal
is a clean, remedy-focused, court-ready motion package that a judge can act on and
an opposing party cannot easily dodge.

This skill applies to enforcement of:
- Final Judgments of Dissolution
- Parenting Plans and Timesharing Orders
- Child Support Orders and Arrears
- Alimony and Spousal Support Orders
- Financial Obligation Orders (asset turnover, deed execution, refinancing)
- Discovery Orders and Case Management Orders
- Mediated Settlement Agreements incorporated into orders
- Any other signed, entered court directive

---

## Step 1: Confirm the Enforceable Order

**Before anything else, identify the exact order being enforced.**

Ask the user to upload or provide:
- [ ] The specific order, judgment, or parenting plan being violated
- [ ] The exact paragraph(s) or provision(s) at issue
- [ ] The date the order was entered
- [ ] Whether the order has ever been modified

Then analyze the operative language:

| Language Type | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Specific date, amount, location, deadline | Enforceable — proceed |
| Conditional or vague ("reasonable," "as agreed") | Risky — flag vagueness before drafting |
| Missing findings or effective date | Flag — may require clarification motion first |
| Parties dispute what it means | Flag — contempt vs. clarification analysis required |

**If the order is vague or ambiguous:**
- Do not draft a contempt motion as if the order is crystal clear
- Note specifically what language is vague and why it creates enforcement risk
- Consider whether a motion to clarify or motion to enforce (non-contempt) is the
  correct vehicle before or instead of contempt
- Advise the user that attorney review is needed before selecting the vehicle

---

## Step 2: Issue Classification

Determine what type of enforcement matter this is. Do not treat everything as contempt.

| Issue Type | Correct Vehicle |
|---|---|
| Clear order + willful noncompliance | Motion for Contempt (civil) |
| Clear order + dispute about compliance | Motion for Enforcement / Clarification |
| Missed support payments, known arrears | Motion for Contempt (support) + arrears accounting |
| Parenting plan interference | Motion for Contempt + makeup time |
| Asset not transferred, deed not executed | Motion for Contempt or Enforcement + turnover relief |
| Discovery order violation | Motion for Contempt (discovery) |
| Order is vague, behavior is frustrating | Motion to Clarify / Modify — NOT contempt |
| Client really wants different terms | Supplemental Petition to Modify — NOT contempt |
| Judgment unpaid but no contempt order | Collection remedies (writ, garnishment, lien) |

**Flag immediately if:**
- The client is actually seeking a modification disguised as enforcement
- The order was never properly served on the other party
- The violation is not clearly willful (circumstances suggest inability to comply)
- The evidence is mostly the client's word against the other party's

---

## Step 3: Build the Contempt Framework

For civil contempt in Florida, the moving party must be able to prove:

1. **A valid, entered court order exists** — signed, entered, specific
2. **The respondent had knowledge of the order** — service, prior compliance, court
   presence, or prior acknowledgment
3. **The respondent had the ability to comply** — financial ability, physical ability,
   access to the thing required
4. **The respondent willfully failed to comply** — not accidental, not impossible,
   not excused

Run a fact-check against each element before drafting:

| Element | Facts Supporting It | Facts Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Valid order | | |
| Knowledge | | |
| Ability to comply | | |
| Willful noncompliance | | |

If any element is weak or missing, note it explicitly under **PROOF GAPS** in the output.

---

## Step 4: Build the Chronology

Convert the client's narrative into a neutral, dated, provable fact sequence.

**Format for each entry:**
`[Date] — [What the order required / what happened / what was said or not said]`

Required chronology categories:
- Date order was entered
- Date(s) compliance was due
- What the order specifically required on each date
- What actually happened (or didn't happen)
- Any notice or demand sent by the client
- Any response or acknowledgment by the other party
- Any partial compliance
- Any communication (texts, emails) acknowledging the obligation
- The current status (still outstanding, partially cured, etc.)

Do not let the client's emotional narrative substitute for a chronology. Reframe it.

---

## Step 5: Evidence Checklist

Generate a targeted evidence checklist based on the violation type.

**For support/financial contempt:**
- [ ] Copy of the order establishing the obligation
- [ ] Payment ledger or account history showing what was paid (or not paid)
- [ ] Bank statements showing deposits/withdrawals
- [ ] Payment receipts or lack thereof
- [ ] Any communications acknowledging the debt
- [ ] Income documentation showing ability to pay
- [ ] Arrears calculation with specific months and amounts

**For parenting plan/timesharing contempt:**
- [ ] Copy of the parenting plan
- [ ] Log or calendar of missed/denied exchanges with dates
- [ ] Text messages or emails showing refusals or interference
- [ ] Communications from the other parent around exchange times
- [ ] School or medical records showing pattern (if relevant)
- [ ] Third-party witness availability (if exchange was witnessed)

**For financial obligation contempt (asset turnover, deed, refinancing):**
- [ ] Copy of order with deadline
- [ ] Correspondence demanding compliance
- [ ] Documentation of non-action (title search, recorded deed status, loan records)
- [ ] Any communications explaining why they haven't complied

**For discovery contempt:**
- [ ] Copy of the discovery order or case management order
- [ ] Proof of service of the discovery requests or order
- [ ] Prior correspondence demanding compliance
- [ ] Log of what has and has not been produced

## Step 6: Remedy Selection

**Every motion must request specific, enforceable relief.** Do not draft a contempt
motion that complains without identifying exactly what the court should order.

Common Florida family law enforcement remedies:

| Remedy | When Applicable |
|---|---|
| Civil contempt finding | Proven willful noncompliance with any order |
| Purge conditions | Amount to pay / act to perform to purge contempt |
| Compliance deadline | Specific date by which action must be taken |
| Makeup timesharing | Denied parenting time — equal makeup provisions |
| Arrears judgment | Support arrears — specific dollar amount |
| Wage deduction order | Support arrears — income withholding |
| Attorney's fees and costs | §61.16 / contempt fees — available for prevailing party |
| Sanctions | Escalating non-compliance, discovery abuse |
| Incarceration (last resort) | Only with ability to comply + purge provision |
| Writ of execution | Judgment on arrears — money judgment enforcement |
| Asset subpoena | Post-judgment collection of financial information |

Identify which remedies apply to the specific facts. Do not include remedies that
are not supported by the record.

## Step 7: Draft the Motion Package

### Standard Motion Structure (Neave Family Law Group, P.A. Style)

```
IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF THE [COUNTY] JUDICIAL CIRCUIT
IN AND FOR [COUNTY] COUNTY, FLORIDA

IN RE: THE MATTER OF:

[PETITIONER FULL NAME],
    Petitioner,
v.
[RESPONDENT FULL NAME],
    Respondent.

Case No.: [CASE NUMBER]
Division: [DIVISION]

MOTION FOR CONTEMPT AND ENFORCEMENT
(OR: MOTION FOR ENFORCEMENT)

COMES NOW Petitioner/Respondent, [CLIENT NAME], by and through the undersigned
[attorney / pro se], and moves this Court for an Order of Contempt and Enforcement,
and in support states:

THE ORDER AT ISSUE
[Numbered paragraphs identifying the enforceable order, its entry date, and the
specific provisions at issue — quote the exact language]

THE VIOLATION
[Numbered paragraphs converting the chronology into factual allegations — each
paragraph one discrete fact, specific dates, specific amounts, specific acts or
omissions]

WILLFULNESS AND ABILITY TO COMPLY
[Numbered paragraphs addressing the other party's knowledge and ability]

HARM SUFFERED
[Numbered paragraphs on impact — financial, parenting, or other harm]

WHEREFORE, [CLIENT NAME] respectfully requests this Court:
[Itemized relief — each remedy as a separate numbered item]

CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
```

### Tone and Drafting Rules
- Direct, factual, remedy-focused
- Numbered paragraphs — one fact per paragraph
- No drama, no characterizations, no adjectives that cannot be proven
- Every allegation tied to a specific date, document, or provable fact
- Every requested remedy tied to a specific, enforceable order
- Proposed order accompanies every major motion

## Step 8: Proposed Order

Draft a proposed order to accompany every motion for contempt or enforcement. The
judge should be able to sign it as presented.

**Proposed order must include:**
- Finding of contempt (if supported)
- Specific act found in violation
- Deadline for compliance / purge
- Dollar amount of arrears (if support)
- Makeup timesharing provisions (if applicable)
- Attorney's fees finding (if sought)
- Purge conditions (if incarceration is sought)

**Proposed order format:**
```
ORDER ON MOTION FOR CONTEMPT AND ENFORCEMENT

THIS CAUSE came before the Court on [DATE] on [CLIENT]'s Motion for Contempt and
Enforcement. The Court, having considered the motion, evidence presented, and argument
of counsel, and being otherwise duly advised, hereby ORDERS AND ADJUDGES:

1. [Finding of contempt / enforcement finding]
2. [Specific compliance required]
3. [Deadline]
4. [Arrears / purge amount if applicable]
5. [Attorney's fees if applicable]
6. [Consequences for further noncompliance]

DONE AND ORDERED in [COUNTY] County, Florida, this ___ day of __________, 20__.

                    ________________________________
                    Circuit Court Judge
```

## Step 9: Hearing Preparation Checklist

When the hearing is approaching, generate this checklist for the client / paralegal:

- [ ] Certified copy of the order being enforced — bring to court
- [ ] Payment ledger / arrears calculation (if support)
- [ ] Exchange log or parenting calendar (if timesharing)
- [ ] Key text messages or emails — printed and organized by date
- [ ] Proposed order — printed and ready to hand up
- [ ] Arrears amount calculated through hearing date
- [ ] Witness list confirmed (if witnesses are needed)
- [ ] Notice of hearing filed and served
- [ ] Client prepped: what they must say, what they must not say
- [ ] Backup copies of all exhibits

## Red Flags — Stop and Flag These

- **Order is vague.** Do not draft a contempt motion against vague language.
  Recommend clarification motion or modification instead. Flag for attorney.

- **Client cannot prove ability to comply.** The other party may have a valid
  inability defense. The motion will fail without addressing this.

- **Violation may be dispute about meaning, not refusal.** If both parties can
  argue the order means something different, it's a clarification problem.

- **Client is really asking for more or different than the order provides.**
  That's modification, not contempt. Flag immediately.

- **Evidence is thin.** Client's word alone is not enough. Identify exactly
  what evidence is missing before drafting.

- **Service of the original order is uncertain.** Cannot prove knowledge without
  it. Identify how knowledge will be shown.
